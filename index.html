01 / The exhibition

A familiar word.
A different hand.

Exhibition concept

Handwriting is easy to read past. We look for a message, a name, a reminder. But what happens when we pause to look at the writing itself?

Ordinary Hands brings everyday handwriting into view through repetition and comparison. The same word returns in different forms: fine lines, dense strokes, rounded letters and uneven rhythms. Here, those differences become the subject.

Curatorial statement

This exhibition asks how familiar aesthetic standards shape the value we give to everyday writing. Selection changes what we notice, but it can also leave things out. A beautiful mark alone cannot tell us who made it or what it originally meant.

Look closely, without treating a style as proof of a writer’s identity. Consider what is visible in a stroke—and what still needs its context.

Curated by Zhaoxi Geng

03 / Cultural context

More than
the words we read.

Writing has a visual life as well as a linguistic one. Different traditions offer ways to look closely, while reminding us that form belongs to a context.

I

Gesture & written form

In Chinese calligraphy, brush pressure, movement and the handling of ink shape a line. The Metropolitan Museum of Art’s account connects these marks to bodily gesture and individual expression within established conventions.

Dawn Delbanco, “Chinese Calligraphy” ↗

The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 2008

II

The handmade page

Medieval European manuscripts brought writing, decorated initials and page ornament together. The handmade book carried knowledge while also being valued as a crafted visual object.

“The Art of the Book in the Middle Ages” ↗

Department of Medieval Art and The Cloisters, 2001

III

Everyday marks, reframed

This exhibition places everyday samples in a space for close attention. It does not present them as historical calligraphy or manuscript art. A display changes how a mark is seen; its writer and original purpose still matter.

When writing becomes an image, what might we lose sight of?
